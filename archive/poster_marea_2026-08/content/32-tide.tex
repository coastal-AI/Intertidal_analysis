\begin{pblock}{The limiting error is the tide}
One systematic error is shared by both estimators. Relief comes out
\term{compressed} — the regression of mapped against surveyed elevation has
slope $0.7$--$0.9$, not $1$ — and the compression worsens
\emphnum{$\approx1.0$ of slope per kilometre} upstream, surviving control for
elevation ($t=-3.9$ to $-6.6$, $p\le0.0002$ across six products). Being
identical in both methods, it cannot live in the estimator. It lives in what
they share: the assumed water level.

\vspace{3mm}
The sigmoid fit is invariant under \emph{affine} maps of the tide axis, which
is a theorem rather than a limit of effort — and it says exactly which
estuarine effects a satellite can recover:

\vspace{2mm}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l r@{}}
\toprule
Estuarine effect & Recoverable? & Measured \\
\midrule
Datum offset            & No — needs a gauge      & --- \\
Constant amplification  & No — needs ground truth & --- \\
Phase lag               & Yes — flood vs.\ ebb    & $\approx0$ \\
\textbf{Damping with range} & Yes — springs vs.\ neaps & \textbf{0.25\,m} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{3mm}
The spring--neap effect alone is twice the RMSE the estimators are separated
by. And the published ensemble tide model does not transfer: its ranking
dataset is 32\,936 points, \emph{all} in Australian waters, so outside that
area the weighting collapses and the ensemble becomes an unweighted mean of
its members — difference 0.0000\,m over 1379 dates.
\end{pblock}

\begin{pblock}{The flat as its own tide gauge \wip}
So stop treating the water level as an input. Let $h_t$ be unknown and solve
it jointly with the elevations: which pixels are wet tells you how high the
water stood, with tens of thousands voting.

\vspace{2mm}

\vspace{2mm}
A scene's level is theoretically readable to \emphnum{5\,mm}, against 140\,mm
for the harmonic model. The obstacle: haze shifts every pixel of a scene
together, which to first order is indistinguishable from the water rising.
Calibrating on \emph{saturated} pixels, which cannot respond to level, removes
it — and agreement with the survey rises from $r=0.845$ to $r=0.890$. Not yet
conclusive, and reported as such.
\end{pblock}
